A sediment core looks continuous from a distance. Up close, it is a chain of
decisions: where to sample, which proxy to trust, how to align depths with time,
and what to do when two signals disagree.

![An abstract diagram of layered sediment bands and sample markers](/media/core-layers.svg)

For our fictional Pukaki Rise record, we keep those decisions beside the
measurements. Each published value can be traced to a sample, each sample to an
interval, and each interval to the chronology version used to interpret it.

## Keep disagreement visible

Agreement between proxies is evidence. Disagreement is also evidence. A useful
record preserves both, along with the judgement that connects them to a claim.
That is slower than flattening everything into one confident line, and much
easier to audit later.
